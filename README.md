#SolarTrack: Arduino-Based Automatic Solar Tracking System

# Overview

**SolarTrack** is an Arduino-based automatic solar tracking system developed to improve the efficiency of solar energy collection by automatically adjusting the orientation of a solar panel according to the direction of maximum sunlight. Conventional fixed solar panels remain in a single position throughout the day, which can reduce energy collection when the angle between the sunlight and the panel surface changes. This project addresses this limitation by introducing an automated tracking mechanism based on light intensity sensing.

The system uses **Light Dependent Resistors (LDRs)** to detect and compare the intensity of sunlight from different directions. An **Arduino microcontroller** continuously reads the sensor values, determines the direction with the highest light intensity, and controls a **servo motor** to rotate the solar panel toward that direction. As the position of the sun changes throughout the day, the system automatically adjusts the panel position to maintain better alignment with the sunlight.

## Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

- To design and develop a low-cost automatic solar tracking system.
- To detect the direction of maximum sunlight using LDR sensors.
- To automatically control the orientation of a solar panel using a servo motor.
- To demonstrate the application of Arduino in renewable energy systems.
- To improve solar energy harvesting compared with a fixed-panel arrangement.
- To develop a simple and scalable prototype suitable for educational and small-scale applications.

## Working Principle

The SolarTrack system operates by continuously monitoring the intensity of sunlight using multiple LDR sensors. The sensors are positioned in such a way that they can detect differences in light intensity from different directions.

The Arduino receives the analog signals generated by the LDR sensors and compares their corresponding light intensity values. When a significant difference is detected between the sensors, the Arduino determines the direction in which the sunlight is stronger. It then sends an appropriate control signal to the servo motor.

The servo motor rotates the solar panel toward the direction of higher light intensity. When the sensor readings become approximately balanced, the panel remains in its current position. This process is repeated continuously, allowing the system to follow the apparent movement of the sun throughout the day.

The developed prototype can be used as an educational platform for demonstrating **solar tracking, embedded systems, sensor-based automation, and renewable energy technologies**. ## Future Improvements

The current prototype can be further enhanced by implementing **dual-axis tracking**, where the panel can move in both horizontal and vertical directions. Additional improvements could include real-time monitoring of solar panel voltage and current, power-generation comparison between fixed and tracking configurations, automatic calibration, weather-resistant mechanical structures, and IoT-based monitoring for remote data collection.

## Conclusion

SolarTrack demonstrates how a combination of **LDR sensors, Arduino control, and servo motor actuation** can be used to create an automated solar tracking mechanism. The project provides a practical example of integrating embedded systems with renewable energy technology and serves as a foundation for developing more advanced and efficient solar tracking solutions.
